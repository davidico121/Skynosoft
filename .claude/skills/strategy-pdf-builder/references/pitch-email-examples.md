# Pitch email style reference

Real examples of the cold pitch email, used as voice and structure
anchors. Study the paragraph splitting (short, one idea per paragraph,
lots of white space) and the shape, not just the words. Add more examples
here as they prove out in real replies.

## Example 1: Castore (welcome email hierarchy)

```
Subject: Your welcome email buries the discount that drives conversions

Oh good!

Your welcome email landed in my inbox yesterday and I noticed something
that's probably costing you conversions on BFCM.

First, your website is genuinely excellent. "Welcome to the Pursuit of
Better," the obsession with precision storytelling, the visual craft. It
builds desire and positions Castore as premium.

But your welcome email buries the discount. People opt in for 15% off,
get your brand story (which is great), then have to scroll all the way
down to find the offer in a black box at the bottom. You're leading with
storytelling when you should be leading with the incentive, then using
the story to justify why it matters.

Right now your email hierarchy is backwards. The CTA that matters (the
discount they came for) is buried. The generic "Explore Castore" button
comes first.

With BFCM weeks away, this is a conversion leak. People are scrolling
past the offer because you've trained them to focus on the brand
narrative instead of the transaction they opted in for.

Ideally, you'd want the 15% off to lead the email (above the fold,
prominent), with the precision obsession story supporting why Castore is
worth that investment. Then everything after that reinforces the offer.

Based on athletic/performance brands I've worked with, restructuring the
welcome email so the offer leads typically unlocks 30-40% more
first-purchase conversions because people get what they came for
immediately, then the brand story keeps them engaged.

If you're open to this before the holidays, I can help rebuild the email
hierarchy and design. Strategy first, then the visual redesign so you see
exactly how it works.

I've already prepared a plan for Castore and just need some details from
you to tailor it better for your audience and goals.

Let's jump on a quick call and I can walk you through it.

Sounds good?

https://calendly.com/david_owoeye/discuss

P.S. The call is not a sales pitch so no need to bring your credit card
along, just an open mind to the possibilities.

Cheers,
David
Skynosoft Ltd.
skynosoft.net
```

**Why this works, structurally:**
- Subject line states the finding, not a generic "quick question."
- Opens with "Oh good!", a real, slightly informal reaction, not "Hi
  [Name]."
- Names something genuinely good about the site before critiquing
  anything, specific details ("the obsession with precision
  storytelling"), not generic flattery.
- States the problem in one blunt sentence, then explains the mechanism
  (why it's backwards, what it trains the customer to do).
- Ties the fix to a real deadline (BFCM) without inventing urgency that
  isn't there.
- The result estimate is explicitly sourced ("based on athletic/
  performance brands I've worked with") and ranged (30 to 40%), never a
  single suspiciously precise number, and never framed as guaranteed.
- The ask is small and specific: a quick call, not "let's work together."
- The PS removes the one real objection (fear of a hard sell) in one
  line.

**Paragraph rhythm**: almost every paragraph is 1 to 3 sentences. No
paragraph exceeds 4. White space does a lot of the work, don't compress
this into dense blocks when writing a new one.

## Example 2: continuing a reply, not a cold open

Use this shape whenever Step 1 says there's already contact with the
lead, most often: an earlier message asked "is your store still
active?", they said yes, and this pitch is the next message in that
same exchange. The example below is a same-thread reply, no new subject
line needed, that's the most common case. If the prior contact was on a
different channel (a DM, a comment, a job post reply), the beats below
stay the same, only the subject changes to reference that channel
specifically instead of "(reply in the existing thread)".

```
Subject: (reply in the existing thread)

Good to hear it's still up and running!

I took a proper look while I had the tab open, and there's something worth flagging before BFCM hits.

Your product pages are clean, the brand comes through clearly, that part's working.

But I signed up for your email list to see what happens after someone opts in, and nothing came. No welcome email, nothing. The only signup field on the site is a bare footer input with no incentive attached either, so most visitors never even see a reason to opt in in the first place.

That matters most right now. A BFCM visitor who doesn't buy on the spot currently has no way back to you at all, no list to land on, no welcome flow to introduce the brand before asking for the sale.

Based on similar brands I've worked with, fixing that one gap (a real incentive on signup, plus a welcome flow that actually sends) typically turns a meaningful chunk of that lost traffic into a list that still earns past BFCM, into Q1.

I put together the exact plan, a day by day build timeline, and what case studies like this usually unlock in the attached PDF.

Want to jump on a quick call and walk through it? Here's my calendar: https://calendly.com/david_owoeye/discuss

Or just reply here if that's easier, this thread already works fine.

P.S. No sales pitch, no need to bring your card, just your questions.

Godwin
Skynosoft Ltd.
skynosoft.net
```

**Why this works, structurally:**
- No "Hi," no re-introduction, no restating who's emailing, the thread
  already established that.
- Opens by directly acknowledging what they said ("Good to hear it's
  still up and running!"), not a generic "Thanks for your reply."
- The bridge line ("I took a proper look while I had the tab open")
  explains naturally why a follow-up audit is happening at all, it's
  the logical next step after confirming the store is active, not a
  non sequitur.
- Everything from the second "But" paragraph onward is identical in
  shape to the cold version (Example 1): what's working, the specific
  problem, the deadline, the fix, the sourced estimate, the attachment,
  the CTA, the PS. Only the opening two beats change.
- The reply-permission line changes too: "this thread already works
  fine" instead of "reply here if that's easier", since they're already
  mid-conversation, not being asked to start one.
